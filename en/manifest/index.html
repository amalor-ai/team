Manifesto

“A bundle of reeds cannot be broken at once, yet a child can break the reeds one by one.”

A bundle of reeds

We live at a time when one person with a laptop and artificial intelligence can do more than a whole department could twenty years ago. And yet, alone, that person is vulnerable.

Alone you sell hours, alone you burn out, alone you cannot reach the big task.

We bind such people together. Three, four, five specialists – with their AI agents beside them. They take on a task that people need and carry it to a result, stage by stage, answering for one another.

This is the Last Generation Team. It is at the centre of everything we do. Everyone else – clients, investors, partners, the organiser – stands around it. And honour and fair pay belong to those who do the work.
What we believe

Seven convictions

We test each of them in practice, and each is independently confirmed by those who study people and teams.

  1. 1

    Together we are stronger than apart

    Within a group, selfishness beats altruism, but altruistic groups beat selfish groups – as the biologists Wilson and Wilson put it. We choose to be such a group.

  2. 2

    We answer for one another

    If one of us is stuck, it is everyone’s task. It is safe to say “I am struggling”. Google studied 180 of its own teams and found exactly this in first place.

  3. 3

    We are equals at one table

    Every voice weighs the same. A group’s intelligence depends not on its smartest member but on how evenly everyone speaks and listens to one another.

  4. 4

    A small team of different people

    The core is three to five people, and one person’s strength covers another’s weakness. Specialists from the network join for a task, and a team that has grown splits rather than sprawls.

  5. 5

    Contribution is visible to all

    Recognition is for what you gave, not for a title. Givers win where what they give is seen and protected.

  6. 6

    We govern what we share ourselves

    The team agrees its rules, watches over them and improves them itself. That is how communities last.

  7. 7

    Benefit to people is the measure of a task

    We take on only what is truly needed, harms no one and someone is willing to pay for. Every task passes through a sieve of seven questions.

Honour and fair pay

80 cents of every dollar go to the team. Always

This is not generosity but structure: everything around exists to serve the centre, not to live off it. Example: a $100,000 project.

Its 20% is as much as each member of a team of four receives: $20,000 per project. The referrer receives 5% of the first project. The team’s income is split equally. How the team lives

Three hours of meetings a week

That is enough to be a team, and it fits alongside a main job.

  1. Mon30 min

    Check-in

    The goal in one sentence. In turn, everyone names their main task and where they need help.

  2. Tue–Thuno meetings

    Work

    A short note in the chat: what is done, where you are stuck. Whoever is stuck gets help.

  3. Fri45 min

    Demo and recognition round

    What we did and learned. Everyone names something valuable another person did.

  4. 6 weeks90 min

    Big retro and pause

    Continue, change course or part ways – we decide together.

  5. Quarter

    Big gathering

    The team confirms roles and lead by what was done, not by sympathy.

The team agreement. The team signs it on day one: we answer for the result together, speak of colleagues with appreciation, decide by consent and, in a dispute, first restate the other side’s view. We reread it at every big retro.
